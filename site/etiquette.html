Dating in Japan: etiquette

What dates in Japan usually look like, and where newcomers trip up. These are common customs, not rules. Younger people tend to be more casual, so follow the person in front of you.

Paying the bill

  • On a first date, the man usually pays, or pays the larger share. Surveys disagree on how strongly women expect it: in one 2024 survey of 100 women, 64 wanted the man to pay more or all of it, while another survey found 58% of women preferred to split. Younger women lean toward splitting.
  • The safe default is to offer to pay, or to pay more. Marriage agency IBJ advises men to pay where they can, because a reputation for being stingy is hard to shake. In a Forbes Japan piece on a survey of women, nearly half said a first-date suggestion to split left a negative impression.
  • Pay at the register, or while she steps away to the restroom. Most Japanese restaurants settle at the register, and paying while she is away means the bill never becomes a conversation.
  • Keep it low-key. Making a show of treating her was a top complaint in a 2026 survey of 100 women (34%).
  • If she offers to pay, decline once, politely. If she insists a second time, let her cover a smaller share, or the next coffee or dessert. She will often say ごちそうさまでした (thank you for the meal).

If you split the bill (割り勘)

  • Never split to the exact yen. In the same 2026 survey, splitting down to the yen was the most-cited bad habit at the register (40%).
  • Round her share down to a clean number and cover the leftover yourself. Use the nearest ¥100 on a small bill, and ¥500 or ¥1,000 on a bigger one.
  • Say it lightly: 今日は多めに出すね (I'll cover a bit more today). Skip the calculator at the table, don't itemize who ate what, and don't hunt for coupons or points.
  • Apps like PayPay can send exact amounts. Don't use them to settle to the yen.

Before and during the date

  • You pick the place and book it. For a first date, choose somewhere you can talk, like a café, lunch or a quiet restaurant. Skip anything built around long queues, such as theme parks.
  • Confirm the plan on LINE the day before. It is considered polite.
  • Be on time. If you are running late, message her right away.
  • Look clean and neat (清潔感, seiketsukan). In a 2018 Mandom survey of 456 people aged 15 to 29, about nine in ten said cleanliness matters in a romantic prospect. Hair, nails, beard, breath and unwrinkled clothes count for more than brands.
  • Don't push drinks. If she wants to head home, take her at her word.
  • Know the last train (終電). Many lines wind down around midnight, so plan the end of the night around it, walk her to the station if you can, and don't stretch the evening past what she is comfortable with.

Messaging on LINE

  • Swap LINE rather than phone numbers. It is the default way to message.
  • Match her pace and message length. If she writes a line or two and you answer with six, it can come across as heavy.
  • A reply within a few hours is common among couples, so a same-day reply is a safe baseline. You don't need to answer instantly.
  • Thank her the same night: 今日はありがとう、楽しかったです。
  • If she goes quiet for a couple of days, don't nudge. Wait, or send one light message about something unrelated.

Getting together

  • Going on dates is not the same as being a couple. A relationship usually starts when someone says it out loud: 告白 (kokuhaku, a confession), often 付き合ってください (please go out with me). Until then, don't assume you are exclusive.
  • Kokuhaku usually comes after a few dates, in person, somewhere quiet. Not on the first date, and not by text. Take 少し考えさせて (let me think about it) as an answer.
  • Start with polite Japanese and her surname plus さん. Switch to first names when she does.
  • Keep physical contact minimal on early dates and respect personal space. Public displays of affection are low-key in Japan.

Useful phrases

ここは出すよ。
I've got this one.
今日は多めに出すね。
I'll cover a bit more today.
ごちそうさまでした。
Thank you for the meal. She may say this to you.
終電は何時?
What time is your last train?
今日はありがとう、楽しかったです。
Thank you for today, I had fun.
付き合ってください。
Please go out with me.
